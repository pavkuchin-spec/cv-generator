# Data Governance strategy & team leadership (Semrush)

## Problem
Data Governance was perceived internally as a policy/compliance function rather than a builder of things analysts and leadership actually used — and the perception had a real cause: an audit of the team's own intake queue found that the large majority of "incoming requests" were in fact self-generated work, not asks from other teams. Leadership wanted a strategy with measurable outcomes, not qualitative claims of value.

## What I did
- Developed a comprehensive two-year Data Governance strategy for the Analytics Division, presented to and approved by VP-level leadership.
- Manage day-to-day operations of a 3-person Data Governance team, setting priorities across the metadata platform, ownership framework, AI knowledge management, and cross-team data agreement workstreams (see companion entries).
- Repositioned how the function reports its value: replaced qualitative claims with monthly-tracked, honestly-scoped metrics (e.g., share of team work originating from outside the team, platform reliability, migration progress against a measured baseline) — each metric chosen specifically because it could be reported starting from zero and trended over time rather than asserted.
- Diagnosed the "we don't know what to ask Data Governance for" perception using hard evidence from the team's own ticket history, and used that finding as the deliberate opening of the repositioning story rather than something to defend against.
- Set an explicit rule for the team going forward: promise a measurement and a delivery date, never an unmeasured improvement — protecting credibility on commitments made to senior stakeholders.

## Scale & outcome
- Two-year strategy approved by VP; shifted the team's external narrative from "governance enforcement" to "hands-on technical enablement" for a 50+ person analytics organization.
- Introduced the team's first monthly leadership-facing metrics reporting.

## Technologies
Stakeholder communication and strategy writing, ticket/work data analysis, cross-functional program management.
